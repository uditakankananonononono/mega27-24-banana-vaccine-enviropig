# Published lettuce processing variation, not banana gut delivery

A [primary lettuce S-HBsAg freeze-drying study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4209752/) evaluated **nine complete process cycles**, with five separate samples in each of two cycles, reporting **17 batch/sample units** overall. The authors say 13/17 had nearly complete VLP preservation and the overall mean VLP recovery was 86%, yet some units dropped below 50% (including within-cycle samples at 48% and 60%). This is a useful positive demonstration that plant-vaccine *processing-stage* dose variability can be quantified. The units are nested, not 17 independent production cycles. The source also warns that total immunoreactive antigen can increase as high as 556% during processing while VLP structure can change, so a total-antigen assay cannot stand in for assembled antigen. In one selected batch used for mice, 50 ng VLP antigen per 1.72 mg lyophilized powder implies 29.07 ng/mg; this is a reported-dose unit check, not observed individual exposure.

One-year storage at 22°C/37°C produced a sharp VLP decline to approximately 8% after three months, while total antigen followed a different course. The article reports 4°C storage differently. None of these measurements is a banana-fruit protein amount or post-gastric intact fraction. The archived HTML, source passages and narrow arithmetic replay in `scripts/audit_lettuce_batch_variation.py`, with test. This adjacent dataset motivates a prespecified stage-aware measurement design, not any intervention or formulation instruction, discovery, comparator win or project gate credit.
