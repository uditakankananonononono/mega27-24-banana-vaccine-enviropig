# Published lettuce case: different denominators at different stages

A [primary oral-vaccine lettuce study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3088802/) reports a useful measurement distinction within one paper: the authors say assembled antigen content fell by **at least 90% when comparing fresh tissue with freeze-dried tissue**, after accounting for dry weight, yet the remaining content in stored powder and tablets was **95–98% stable after one year at room temperature**. The article also reports about **2.3 μg per tablet** and describes three repeated assays for leaves versus ten for powder/tablets. These are author-reported values from different contexts and denominators, not a newly measured time series. The archived article XML, byte hash and paragraph-extraction script/test pin the claims.

This makes a narrower, valid published-data analysis question: *where in the published reporting chain is retention measured, and which denominator is used?* High retention during storage of the final product does not undo a prior processing-stage loss. Nor does either number measure post-digestion survival. One cannot multiply the reported ranges into a universal delivered-dose estimate because assay definitions, starting material, and paired replicate values are not all available here. Repeated ELISA assays do not equal ten independently sampled plants. The example supplies a concrete source-grounded distinction and supports an evidence-checking tool, but no cross-study prediction, plant-level variation estimate, benchmark or new discovery yet.

A prospective comparison would independently screen more existing oral-vaccine primary papers for numerator, denominator, stage, units, assay type and biological versus technical repeats. Only papers with compatible measured outcomes can enter a same-task, grouped holdout benchmark. This is published-design evidence analysis, with no construct or protocol recommendation.
