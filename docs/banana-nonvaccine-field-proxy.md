# Banana fruit field variability is not antigen variability, 2026-09-28

The [2017 field study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5362681/) measured fruit pro-vitamin A carotenoids in banana. Its 244 transformed lines initially had one field plant per line, while a later replicated selection used suckers. Across two field trials, the reported *wild-type fruit* means ranged from 1.0 to 8.1 µg/g dry weight beta-carotene equivalents in different harvest months, an **8.1-fold ratio of two context-specific extrema**. This is not a within-fruit or plant-to-plant coefficient of variation.

A [later field study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13110172/) explicitly assessed fruit trait stability over three successive vegetative generations, with a replicated design including 27 selected transformed and five wild-type lines. Those actual field measurements make banana fruit a plausible matrix for studying variability, but the measured analyte is **carotenoid**, not vaccine antigen. The early single-plant-per-line design cannot separate individual line effects from plant variability; the later selected-line crop is not an independent vaccine validation cohort. These papers cannot fill the locked post-digestion intact-antigen endpoint, estimate oral vaccine dose, or support a biological construct recommendation.
